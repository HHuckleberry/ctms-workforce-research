Within-tier pay compression

Does the size of a person's raise correlate with where they already sat in their rate tier's pay range? Tested against two more mundane explanations first: a concurrent rate-tier/pay-plan change landing the same month (too few people, ~2/year, to explain the spread), and duty-station variation (people in the same city still show wide spread from 2024 on, so it isn't locality tables moving unevenly either). DC pay plan only — DL's per-tier samples are too thin to read a slope from. Each dot is one person; the line is the fitted trend across all DC people that year.

2023 shows no relationship at all — essentially everyone got the same flat percentage regardless of where they sat in their tier. Starting in 2024, that flips: people lower in their tier's range consistently got bigger raises than people near the top, and by 2025 the top of several tiers isn't just getting smaller raises — a meaningful share got exactly 0%, most sharply in RATE 05 (5 of the top 7 people that year). That's consistent with a real ceiling, not just easing off. Two readings fit the data equally well and can't be told apart without a performance-rating field OPM's public data doesn't include: either a genuine cap on what a top-of-tier person can be paid without an administrative tier promotion — plausible, since CTMS positions are paid under 6 U.S.C. 658 and DHS's own public materials only ever post "typical starting" ranges per level, never a stated ceiling — or high performers stuck at a tier's practical max going unrewarded until their next promotion. Both look identical in pay data alone.
