Methodology & caveats

Source. OPM's public open-data API (data.opm.gov), monthly employment / accessions / separations snapshots, no authentication required. Population = pay plan DC or DL intersected with the known DHS-only cybersecurity occupational-series family. The updater audits every DC/DL record by series and warns if another code appears.

DHS-CS context. The employee population uses DC/DL records in the DHS-only cybersecurity series family (2212, 2213, 2218, 2221, 2224–2226, 2228–2230). Current opportunities, technical capabilities, career tracks, and application guidance are available at the official DHS Cybersecurity Service portal.

First observed is not an exact CTMS start date. service_computation_date_leave is total creditable federal service and can predate CTMS by years. The report's cohort month is the first monthly snapshot in which a person appears in the CTMS-filtered population. Except for the left-censored first month of the dataset, that means the person appeared sometime after the preceding snapshot and by the current snapshot; it does not establish the precise appointment date. Cohort, observed-span, and retention calculations use this first-observed month.

Individual tracking is inferred, not exact. The public data carries no employee ID. Each record is keyed on service computation date + duty station + age bracket + education level — unique within a single month, but the same real person can drift across that key (an age bracket ticks over, a relocation, a degree finished). A linking pass reconnects drifted records when they share a service date, the same subelement, and no more than one other field differs, while refusing to merge records whose active months overlap (concurrent people, not one person). Two different people can share a service-computation date by coincidence — CTMS hires in cohorts, so a whole batch can start the same day — so single-field matches on service date alone are deliberately not merged. A second pass then checks every resulting group as a whole, not just the individual links that built it: pairwise-valid links can still chain two unrelated people together transitively through a shared intermediate record, so any group that wouldn't survive a full pairwise check gets split back into separate people rather than risk a false merge.

Reproduce it. opm_ctms_pull.py pulls the raw snapshots, ctms_track_individuals.py builds the individual timelines and events, ctms_report_data.py rolls everything in this page up — all three in CTMS Docs/opm_ctms_pull/.
